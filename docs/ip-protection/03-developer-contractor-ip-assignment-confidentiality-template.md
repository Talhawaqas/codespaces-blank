### 1. SCOPE OF WORK

1.1 The Contributor is engaged to provide services described as: **[BRIEF DESCRIPTION — e.g., "software development on the Inaya Business Workspace" / "smart contract engineering" / "documentation"]**, under a separate engagement/employment arrangement dated **[DATE]** (if any).

### 2. IP ASSIGNMENT

2.1 The Contributor hereby assigns to the Company, with full title guarantee, all right, title, and interest — present and future, worldwide — in and to any Intellectual Property Rights (as defined in the Founder IP Assignment Agreement) created, conceived, developed, or contributed by the Contributor in the course of, or in connection with, work performed for the Company or the Inaya Project, including code, designs, documentation, and any other original work product.

2.2 This assignment does **not** apply to:
(a) work clearly outside the scope of the Contributor's engagement with the Company, and unrelated to it;
(b) Pre-Existing IP disclosed in **Schedule A**;
(c) any third-party open-source components used under their own license, which remain governed by that license (see Clause 5).

2.3 Where full legal assignment of a specific right is not possible under applicable law, the Contributor grants the Company an exclusive, perpetual, irrevocable, worldwide, royalty-free, sublicensable license to that right instead.

### 3. MORAL RIGHTS

3.1 To the extent permitted by law, the Contributor waives all moral rights in the assigned work product.

### 4. WAIVER FOR OPEN-SOURCE / VOLUNTEER CONTRIBUTORS

4.1 Where the Contributor is an open-source or volunteer contributor (not paid staff or a formally engaged contractor), this Agreement applies only to contributions actually submitted and merged into a Company-controlled repository, and the Contributor confirms they have the right to make such contributions.

### 5. THIRD-PARTY / OPEN-SOURCE COMPONENTS

5.1 The Contributor will disclose to the Company any third-party or open-source software incorporated into their work, including its license terms, before submission — particularly any component under a copyleft license (e.g., GPL) that could affect how the surrounding code may be distributed or licensed.

### 6. CONFIDENTIALITY

6.1 The Contributor agrees to keep confidential all non-public technical and business information relating to the Company and the Inaya Project that they access or are told about — including source code, unreleased features, security mechanisms, business strategy, and user/customer data — both during and after their engagement.

6.2 This obligation does not apply to information that:
(a) is or becomes publicly available through no fault of the Contributor;
(b) was already known to the Contributor before receiving it from the Company, as shown by written records;
(c) is required to be disclosed by law or a valid court order, provided the Company is notified first where legally permitted.

6.3 The Contributor will not use confidential information for any purpose other than performing their work for the Company.

### 7. RETURN OF MATERIALS

7.1 On request or on termination of the engagement, the Contributor will return or securely delete all Company materials, credentials, and confidential information in their possession, and confirm this in writing.

### 8. FURTHER ASSURANCES

8.1 The Contributor agrees to execute further documents reasonably requested by the Company to perfect this assignment, including for copyright or patent registration purposes, both during and after the engagement.

### 9. WARRANTIES

9.1 The Contributor warrants that their work product is original (except for disclosed third-party components), does not knowingly infringe any third party's rights, and is not subject to any conflicting obligation to another party.

### 10. GOVERNING LAW

10.1 This Agreement is governed by the laws of Pakistan. The courts of **[CITY]**, Pakistan shall have exclusive jurisdiction.

**Drafting notes for whoever finalizes this (delete before use):**
- This template is deliberately written to cover employees, contractors, *and* open-source/volunteer contributors with one document plus a checkbox, per the SOW's own instruction to cover "founders, developers, contractors and other contributors" without over-engineering separate documents for each. Counsel may still recommend splitting it, especially for employees where Pakistani labour law considerations (not just IP law) apply.
- Clause 5 exists because this codebase genuinely depends on third-party open-source packages (npm/cargo dependencies throughout) — a contributor's own undisclosed inclusion of copyleft-licensed code is a real, practical risk this clause is meant to catch early, not just a boilerplate formality.
- For contributors who are not Pakistani residents, consider whether Clause 10's governing-law choice is realistically enforceable against them, and whether a different dispute-resolution mechanism (e.g., arbitration) is more practical.
- A short-form version of just Clauses 2, 6, and 9 may be more realistic to get signed quickly from a large number of small/one-off contributors, with this full version reserved for anyone with sustained or sensitive access.
