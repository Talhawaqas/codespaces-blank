## What it is

Hospitality OS is an industry edition of the Business Workspace. It adds the day-to-day workflows of a food business and connects them to the modules you already have: CRM, Inventory, Procurement, HR, Finance, Campaigns and Automations.

Choose **Restaurant / cafe / catering (Hospitality OS)** as your business type when you create the company, or later in Settings.

It is not a point-of-sale system. It doesn't take payments, run a kitchen display or connect to delivery apps.

## Getting started

1. **Add a location** in Hospitality > Settings: name, time zone, currency, opening hours and your reservation policy.
2. **Add seating areas and tables**, with the smallest and largest party each table takes. Mark tables that can be pushed together.
3. **Give your team roles** in Settings > Roles & access. With more than one location, tick the locations each person works at.
4. **Link a stock location** if you want stock, wastage and reorder on the Stock tab.

## Roles

| Role | What they can do |
|---|---|
| Manager | Everything at their locations, including approvals and reports |
| Host | Reservations, waitlist and guest notes |
| Service | Today's reservations (no contact details or staff notes), seating, checklists |
| Kitchen | Menu drafts, recipes, wastage, checklists |
| Stock | Counts, wastage, reorder requests, deliveries |

Owners and admins can do everything at every location.

## Reservations

- Take a booking by phone, in person or as a walk-in. The system picks the smallest free table that fits, or you choose one.
- Two bookings can't hold the same table at the same time, and you can set the most guests you'll take at once.
- A booking moves through confirmed, arrived, seated and completed, or is cancelled, declined or marked as a no-show.
- Guests get a confirmation email, a reminder, and an email if the booking changes or is cancelled. Each reservation shows whether the email was handed to the mail provider.
- The **Floor** view shows every table as free, reserved or seated, with what's next.

### Online booking page

Turn it on per location in Settings and choose a web address. Guests pick a party size, a date and a free time, and get a link to view or cancel their booking. The page shows only your name, address, hours and free times. It never takes card details.

### Waitlist

Add walk-ins who are waiting. When a table that fits them is free, the waitlist says so and you can seat them in one step. The system doesn't estimate waiting times; you can note the time you quoted.

## Guests

Guests are your CRM contacts. A booking with an email address links to the existing contact or creates one. A booking that only shares a phone number with an existing contact is never merged automatically: a manager reviews it. Completed visits are added to the contact's history.

Marketing consent is separate from the booking. A guest is only opted in if they ticked the box.

## Menu and recipes

- Menus, categories and items with prices, options (sizes, add-ons), dietary labels and allergens.
- Kitchen staff draft items. A manager reviews the allergen information and publishes. Price and allergen changes on a published item are manager-only and recorded.
- A recipe lists ingredients from Inventory with quantities. It shows stock on hand and how many servings that covers.
- An estimated cost per serving appears only when every ingredient has a purchase price.

Allergen and dietary information is entered by the restaurant, which remains responsible for ingredient accuracy and cross-contamination practices.

## Stock

- **Stock & reorder:** stock at the location, low-stock flags and a suggested order quantity. One click drafts a purchase request, which still has to be submitted and approved in Procurement.
- **Wastage:** record what was lost and why. Above a quantity you set, a manager other than the person who recorded it has to approve before stock is reduced.
- **Stock counts:** enter what you counted. Small differences are applied; large ones wait for a manager.
- **Deliveries:** tick the delivery checks and record anything short or damaged against the purchase order.

Stock is not reduced when dishes are sold, because no point-of-sale system is connected.

## Operations

- **Staff shifts:** build the week, publish it, and staff are notified. Staff can mark days they can't work and ask to swap a shift; a manager decides.
- **Checklists:** opening and closing checklists with due times. Items can require a photo or document.
- **Incidents:** record guest complaints, equipment faults, shortages and safety incidents, assign them and resolve them.

## Campaigns, automations and the assistant

- Guest campaigns are sent from **Campaigns**, with one unsubscribe list and an approval step. Use the "Guest visits" audience rule to reach guests who have, or haven't, visited recently.
- **Automations** has four hospitality templates: new reservation alert, no-show follow-up, delivery discrepancy alert and guest feedback follow-up.
- The **assistant** can answer questions such as "what reservations are expected in the next two hours?" or "which ingredients are low?". It can't book, cancel, change stock or send anything.

## Reports

Reservations by status, guests served, no-show and cancellation rates, how bookings came in, waitlist, wastage, stock-count differences, purchase orders by supplier, checklists and incidents, for any date range and location. A figure with too little data says so instead of showing zero.
